# Why the product is fixed

A factory is organised around repetition. The arcade repeats an output shape:
every shift, one new game, always three files, always in the catalogue. The
machinery repeats a different shape: one new machine, four files, a fixed API,
a manual. In both, the product is new every time and the envelope never moves.

The whetstone inverts that - and stays a factory. Here it is the *product*
that never moves: one game, the same one, every shift. What repeats is the
pass: play the game, read the ledger of every pass before, name the dullest
facet, make one improvement, play again to prove it, write it down. A
whetstone does not make knives. It takes the one blade it is given and makes
it sharper, pass after pass, and every pass travels the same path across the
stone.

That path is the fixed shape. A shift may not do two things; it may not do a
vague thing; it may not do a thing it cannot name a facet for. It must play
before it may touch, and play again before it may claim. The ledger is what
makes the repetition honest: each entry names the facet, the grievance found
by playing, the change, and the proof - so the next shift inherits not just a
better game but the record of *how* it got better, and does not hone the same
edge twice while another goes dull. The facets rotate because the ledger will
not take the same facet two passes running.

The ledger is not the only record. Every pass leaves the whole blade behind:
a pass copies the latest version to the next numbered folder and hones the
copy, so `versions/0` is the game as cast and `versions/n` is the game after
pass n - all of them playable, none of them ever touched again. Written
history says what changed; the shelf lets you feel it, one version against
the next. A claim of "sharper" that cannot survive playing the two versions
back to back is not a pass, and the shelf is what makes that comparison
possible forever, not just on the shift that made it.

The obvious objection is the one the arcade's philosophy makes about
workshops: a different job every morning is not a process you can improve. But
the whetstone's mornings are not different jobs. The job is identical every
shift - one pass, same order, same gate, same entry to write - and only the
facet in the jaws changes, drawn from a fixed list the way the arcade draws a
new genre from an open one. Failures compare: a pass whose proof does not
hold, a pass that broke the blade, a pass that widened into three - each
becomes a rule, and the gate remembers the rule so no shift has to.

There is one rule above the others: **never break the blade.** The game is
playable at the end of every shift, from the first pass to the thousandth, or
the shift does not land. A factory that makes new things can ship a dud and
move on; a factory that holds one thing has no such luxury - the dud would
*be* the product. So the probe plays the game before any shift may end, and a
pass that cannot prove the game still runs is a pass that never happened.

That's the whole idea. Fix the product so the pass repeats; a pass that
repeats is one you can improve; and make the model play what it hones, so
that "sharper" is something it felt rather than something it asserts.
